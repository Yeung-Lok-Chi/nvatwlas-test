The noun is a decision point.

A small corpus study of how English nominalizations become Chinese verbal actions in film subtitles.

On this page OriginBackgroundGapQuestionsMethodAnalysisConclusionReflection

1. Origin

I chose this topic after comparing the department’s NV Atlas with the course example. Both show the same broad concern—English nominalization and Chinese verbalization—but they approach it differently. NV Atlas offers a large searchable collection; the class project requires a smaller, explainable system. That contrast gave me a practical question: can a 500-pair sample make the choices inside translation easier to see?

I was also interested in a familiar subtitle pattern. A noun such as attention can be reproduced as the Chinese noun-like form 注意, but it can also become actions such as 稍息, 知道, or 重视. The translation is not a mechanical substitution. It depends on the scene, the speech act, and what the target language can say naturally.

2. Background

The project starts from the contrast between an English style that often packages events as nouns and a Chinese style that often expresses them through verbs. In translation studies, this is related to Vinay and Darbelnet’s translation procedures, particularly transposition, and to broader discussions of “noun-heavy” English and “verb-heavy” Chinese.

The supplied CSV already identifies an English nominal and a Chinese rendering for each sentence pair. That gives the project a useful unit of analysis: a short subtitle pair and one aligned nominal–verbal relationship inside it. The corpus is not a general sample of all English or Chinese; it is a sample of translated film dialogue.

3. Gap

A searchable corpus can show that several translations exist, but a list of matches does not by itself explain how those alternatives differ. Large dashboards can also make it difficult to move from a pattern back to the sentence that produced it. My project therefore focuses on the path from an English nominal to multiple Chinese renderings, while keeping the original sentence pair visible.

There is a second gap in the supplied data: it does not include a documented sampling seed or a person-verified flag. I have not invented either one. The website records the public record reconciliation and treats the 500 IDs as a fixed analytical sample. That limitation matters: the category shares describe this sample, not the whole NV Atlas.

4. Questions

  1. Which English nominal types appear with the widest range of Chinese renderings in this sample?
  2. How are those renderings distributed across five functional categories?
  3. What can a bilingual keyword-in-context view reveal that a search result list cannot?
  4. Can linked visualizations help a reader move from an aggregate pattern back to verifiable evidence?

5. Method

Source and sample

The data comes from the supplied NVAtlas-AI-Agent.csv, whose records are identified as OpenSubtitles v2024 material through the public NV Atlas. The CSV contains 500 unique sentence IDs and 195 unique English nominal lemmas. I preserved the raw file and created a normalized website dataset; the original CSV’s SHA-256 digest is recorded in data/build_report.json.

The 500 IDs are not consecutive and are not reproducible from the random seeds I tested. Because no seed is documented, this is a frozen analytical sample, not a probability sample. I therefore do not present category percentages as estimates of the full NV Atlas.

Source reconciliation

I compared every record with the public NV Atlas search payload. All 500 IDs were found. Using Unicode NFKC and whitespace normalization, 481 of 500 sentence pairs matched; 19 differ in Chinese character form, punctuation, or wording. These are logged in data/audit.json and are not silently corrected.

Loading reconciliation report…

Verification sample

A fixed sample of 50 IDs was drawn with random.Random(20261105).sample from the 500 IDs. The sample is available for the required human check and is shown through the 50-row audit sample filter. Programmatic reconciliation is not the same as personal verification, so the final student review must still read each pair against the source and set human_checked only after approval.

Classification

I classified the 195 nominal types using the five-category codebook published on this site. Assignments are made at the lemma-type level and then applied to all records containing that type. This improves consistency, but it also means that a context-sensitive exception is not separately coded. Boundary cases are discussed in the codebook.

Codebook log

Initial draft: a rough distinction between “direct action” and “communication.” Test revision: the draft was too broad and mixed nouns that name a communicative act with nouns that describe an internal state. Final revision: five functional categories—direct verbalization, event/process, speech/communication, mental/emotional, and relational/state—were fixed before the visualizations were built. A computational check caught 13 observed lemmas omitted from my first mapping; I corrected the map rather than allowing those records to fall into a default category silently.

6. Analysis

The figures below are generated from the same corpus.json that feeds the explorer. The analysis refers to the four named views in the site.

Category composition

Direct verbalization is the largest category in this sample (164 records, 32.8%), followed by event/process (112, 22.4%), mental/emotional (104, 20.8%), speech/communication (85, 17.0%), and relational/state (35, 7.0%). This does not show that Chinese prefers direct verbs in general. It shows that the supplied sample is dominated by sentence pairs where a nominal was explicitly paired with a relatively direct verbal rendering.

Nominal–verbal paths

The Relational view makes polysemy visible. Attention is the largest hub: 45 records and four distinct renderings, led by 注意 (42), with 稍息, 知道, and 重视 as context-specific alternatives. Permission is another useful case: 17 records and five surface forms in this data, mainly 允许, with 批准, 准许, 获准, and a traditional-character variant. These are not automatically five different translation strategies; they are evidence that one lemma can participate in several contextual choices.

Bilingual KWIC

The KWIC view answers a different question from the network: what does the surrounding sentence actually do? For example, the record Attention! → 稍息 is not a generic rendering of the noun “attention.” It is a military command. Attention, please. → 请注意 is a public address. The same English key term is stable; its speech function is not.

Evidence table

The table keeps IDs, source text, target text, category, and verification status in one place. Clicking a row carries its term and Chinese rendering back to the other views. The table is therefore both a data grid and a check on the charts: a user can inspect the records behind any selected term rather than trusting a visual total.

What the charts do not prove

Several records contain translations that are nouns, adjectives, or fixed expressions rather than verbs. The Chinese_Verb field is the project’s analytical label, not a part-of-speech proof. Shared subtitles also repeat dialogue, and the sample includes 12 repeated source sentences among its 500 records. For these reasons, the result should be read as a qualitative map of possible shifts, not as a frequency study of Chinese grammar.

7. Conclusion

The project achieves a working static corpus explorer: 500 traceable records, five documented categories, a linked network and treemap, a bilingual KWIC view, a sortable table, CSV export, URL-persisted search state, and an English–Traditional Chinese interface. It also makes the analytical limitation visible. The most useful finding is not a single percentage; it is that the same English nominal can be a label, a command, an action, or a state depending on the scene.

8. Reflection

The hardest part was not drawing a chart. It was deciding what should be counted as a category and what should remain a caveat. An AI agent can generate a coherent-looking taxonomy very quickly, but coherence is not the same as a defensible decision rule. I learned to check the number of records, the unique terms, the duplicates, and the source variants before trusting the visual summary.

AI was useful for drafting code, testing edge cases, extracting the public record set, and proposing interaction patterns. It could not decide whether a particular subtitle rendering is a good translation, and it could not honestly mark records as human_checked without a person reviewing them. I would improve the project by documenting a reproducible sample from the start, adding a full sentence-level human audit, and classifying occasional context-sensitive exceptions rather than assigning every occurrence of a lemma the same category.

1. 緣起

我比較翻譯系 NV Atlas 與課程示例網站後選擇了這個題目。兩者都關注英文名詞化與中文動詞化，但取向不同：NV Atlas 是大型可搜尋語料庫，而課程專題要求較小、可解釋的系統。這個對照帶來一個實際問題：500 對樣本能否令翻譯過程中的選擇更容易被看見？

我也留意到字幕中常見的現象。例如英文名詞 attention 可以譯成較接近名詞形式的「注意」，也可以依情境變成「稍息」、「知道」或「重視」。翻譯不是機械替換，而是受場景、言語行為及譯入語自然表達方式影響的決定。

2. 背景

本項目從英文常把事件包裝成名詞、中文常以動詞表達的差異出發。這與 Vinay 和 Darbelnet 的翻譯程序，尤其「詞性轉換」有關，也涉及「英文重名詞、中文重動詞」的討論。

提供的 CSV 已為每對句子標出一個英文名詞及其中文譯法，因此分析單位可界定為「字幕句對中的一組名詞—動詞對應」。這批語料並非英文或中文的普遍樣本，而是翻譯電影對白的樣本。

3. 研究缺口

可搜尋語料庫能顯示多種譯法，但單看搜尋結果，未必能解釋各譯法在情境中的差異；大型儀表板也可能令人難以由整體模式返回原始句對。本項目因此集中呈現「英文名詞到多個中文譯法」的路徑，同時保留原句與譯句。

提供的資料另有一個缺口：沒有記錄抽樣種子，也沒有由人手完成的核對標記。我沒有虛構這些資料。網站記錄了與公開記錄的核對結果，並把 500 個編號視為固定的分析樣本。因此，類別比例只描述這個樣本，不能推論至整個 NV Atlas。

4. 研究問題

  1. 哪些英文名詞類型在本樣本中有最多不同的中文譯法？
  2. 這些譯法如何分佈在五個功能類別中？
  3. 雙語關鍵詞語境視圖能顯示搜尋結果列表以外的甚麼？
  4. 互相連結的視覺化能否幫助讀者由整體模式返回可核查的證據？

5. 方法

來源與樣本

資料來自提供的 NVAtlas-AI-Agent.csv；這些記錄透過公開 NV Atlas 標示為 OpenSubtitles v2024 語料。CSV 有 500 個不重複句子編號及 195 個不重複英文名詞詞元。我保留原始檔案，另建立網站用的正規化資料，並在 data/build_report.json 記錄原 CSV 的 SHA-256 摘要。

500 個編號並非連續，且不能以我測試過的隨機種子重現。由於沒有記錄抽樣種子，這是固定的分析樣本，不是概率樣本。因此，我不會把類別百分比當作整個 NV Atlas 的估計。

來源核對

我把每一筆記錄與公開 NV Atlas 搜尋頁所載資料比較。500 個編號全部找到；使用 Unicode NFKC 及空白正規化後，481 對句對相符，另外 19 對在漢字形體、標點或措辭上有差異。這些差異記錄於 data/audit.json，沒有被暗中修改。

正在載入核對報告…

核對樣本

我以 random.Random(20261105).sample 從 500 個編號中抽取固定的 50 個，供課程要求的人工核對，並可透過「50 筆核對樣本」篩選查看。程式核對不等於個人核對，因此最終仍須由學生逐項閱讀來源並批准後，才可把 human_checked 設為「是」。

分類

我把 195 個名詞類型按網站上的五類分類手冊歸類。分類在詞元層完成，再套用到所有含該詞元的記錄，以提升一致性；但同一詞元在不同情境中的例外不會另設標記。邊界個案在分類手冊中說明。

分類手冊修訂記錄

初稿只粗略區分「直接動作」與「溝通」，測試後發現範圍過闊，並混淆了言語行為與內在心理狀態。最終版本在建立視覺化前固定為五類：直接動詞化、事件／過程、言語／溝通、心理／情感、關係／狀態。程式檢查發現初版映射遺漏 13 個實際出現的詞元；我更正映射，而不是讓資料靜默落入預設類別。

6. 分析

以下圖表與探索器均由同一份 corpus.json 產生。分析會引用網站內四個具名視圖。

類別組成

本樣本中直接動詞化最多（164 筆，32.8%），其次為事件／過程（112 筆，22.4%）、心理／情感（104 筆，20.8%）、言語／溝通（85 筆，17.0%）及關係／狀態（35 筆，7.0%）。這不表示中文普遍偏好直接動詞，而是顯示提供的樣本以名詞與較直接動詞譯法配對的句對為主。

名詞與動詞路徑

關係視圖令一詞多譯的情况更明顯。Attention 是最大的樞紐：45 筆記錄、四種譯法，以「注意」為主（42），另有「稍息」、「知道」和「重視」。Permission 亦值得注意：17 筆記錄中出現五種形式，主要是「允許」，另有「批准」、「准許」、「獲准」及繁體字形變體。這些形式不必然代表五種不同的翻譯策略，而是顯示同一詞元可參與多種情境選擇。

雙語關鍵詞語境

關鍵詞語境視圖回答另一個問題：周圍句子實際在做甚麼？例如 Attention! →「稍息」並不是名詞「attention」的通用譯法，而是軍事口令；Attention, please. →「請注意」則是公共廣播。英文關鍵詞相同，言語功能卻不同。

證據表

表格把句子編號、原文、譯文、類別及核對狀態放在同一處。點擊任何一行，會把該行的名詞及中文譯法帶回其他視圖。因此，表格既是資料表，也是圖表的核對工具：讀者可查看任何已選名詞背後的記錄，而不必只信賴圖表的總數。

圖表不能證明甚麼

部分記錄的中文譯法其實是名詞、形容詞或固定表達，而非動詞。Chinese_Verb 是本項目的分析標籤，不是詞性證明。字幕來源亦可能重複對白；樣本 500 筆中有 12 個重複原句。因此，結果應理解為「可能轉換路徑的質性地圖」，而非中文語法頻率研究。

7. 結論

本項目完成一個可運作的靜態語料探索器：500 筆可追溯記錄、五個公開類別、互相連結的網絡圖與矩形樹圖、雙語關鍵詞語境、可排序表格、CSV 匯出、保存在網址中的搜尋狀態，以及英文／繁體中文介面。它同時公開分析限制。最重要的發現不是某個百分比，而是同一英文名詞可隨場景成為標籤、命令、行動或狀態。

8. 反思

最困難的不是畫圖，而是決定甚麼應算作類別、甚麼應保留為限制。AI 代理能迅速生成看似完整的分類，但「一致」不等於「有辯護力」。我學會先核查記錄數、不重複詞元、重複句子及來源差異，再相信視覺摘要。

AI 有助起草程式、測試邊界情況、抽取公開記錄及提出互動方式；但 AI 不能判斷某個字幕譯法是否好的翻譯，也不能在沒有人工閱讀的情況下誠實地把記錄標為 human_checked。若能重做，我會由一開始記錄可重現的抽樣方法、完成完整的句級人工核對，並為個別情境例外另設類別，而不是把同一詞元的所有出現都歸入同一類。
